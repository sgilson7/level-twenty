# Plan

Each decision, and the words in the brief it answers. Approved in advance (BRIEF.md, Autonomy). P12 to P15 answer Sam's second request, P16 to P20 the third and its follow-ups (BRIEF.md).

| # | Decision | Answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | A fight is decided in full the moment it starts (`battle::fight(teams, seed)`), and the page plays the recorded turns back. Nothing about a fight depends on the page's timing, and the same seed and crews give the same fight on every machine. | "auto battler", "3v3 fights" |
| P2 | Turns come from an attack bar: every unit's gauge fills by its speed, and the fullest gauge at 1000 acts (ties: side 0, then the lower slot). A unit uses its skill when its cooldown is up and attacks otherwise. | "Kind of like Summoners wars"; speed is one of the three stats |
| P3 | Three kinds, each strong against one: melee beats ranged, ranged beats magic, magic beats melee. A hit with the edge does 150%, against it 70%. A unit's usual target is an enemy it has the edge over, then the one with least health. | "Melee weak to magic, magic weak to ranged, ranged weak to melee" |
| P4 | Units have exactly three stats, HP, attack and speed, each a base plus a fixed amount per level, to a cap of 20. No defence stat: the brief names three. | "levels give more stats (attack, hp, speed)", "lvl 20" |
| P5 | Twelve units, four per kind, each with one skill from eight effects (strike, stun, sweep, heal, drain, snipe, double, haste). Three starters (one of each kind) at account level 1; the other nine unlock one per account level from 2 to 10. | "a level out of combat that determines which units you can use" |
| P6 | Account XP and unit XP come from fights: the campaign pays both (first clear pays double account XP); random pays the account only, because its units are not yours; online pays both. | "all units have xp and levels" |
| P7 | The save lives in the browser (`localStorage`), written by the core as JSON and read back by it. A save the core cannot read is a new player, never an error. Levels are clamped and a crew that no longer works is replaced by the starters. | levels must persist to mean anything |
| P8 | Campaign: three parts of town, five fights each, in order; stars count your units standing at the end. Random: both crews drawn from the whole roster, locked units included, at levels within two of the account level. | "campaign style battle mode, or a completely random game mode" |
| P9 | Online 1v1 reuses Ribbit King's and Raiders of the Lost Mart's transport (`web/net/`, Trystero over public Nostr relays, or pasted codes) unchanged but for names. Because P1 makes a fight a pure function of two crews and a seed, the browsers swap crews and the host sends a seed and the fight's fingerprint; there is no lockstep or rollback to keep. The joiner sees the same fight from its own seat (`Record::mirrored`) and reports a fingerprint mismatch instead of showing it. | "Could have online 1v1 mode as well" |
| P10 | Every string a player reads is in `data/copy.json`; unit and campaign text in `data/units.json` and `data/campaign.json`; playback timings in `data/display.json`. The core returns copy keys for errors and every number the page draws, percentages included. | Builder D3, D5 |
| P11 | Art: TikZ through Raiders' `tools/art.py` and `artlib.py`: twelve portraits, three kind icons, the help triangle, three backdrops and the title. Chum 3000 (the mech cat), Jetpack June and Biscuit (the poodle) and Exo Ed (the sweaty exosuit) come from Sam_Comm_final.png. | "use the tikz latex prompt", "sam comm final as an inspiration image" |
| P12 | Three skills per unit, learned at levels 1, 5 and 10 (`data/units.json`). The player picks the one each unit fields on the Crew screen; the core refuses a skill the unit has not learned, a save or an online crew that names one, and a fight uses only the picked skill. Campaign enemies field the last skill their level opens; random crews a random opened one. Two effects were added so thirty-six skills could differ: Mend (heals every ally) and Slow (pushes the target's turn back). | "each character should have 3 abilities you can choose from for battle, that you get as you level them up" |
| P13 | A 3D stage between the two columns of tiles, drawn with WebGL 2 by Raiders of the Lost Mart's renderer. The core works out every frame from the fight record and a playback clock (`stage.rs`, `figure.rs`): melee units run in and swing, ranged units fire a shot that arcs to the target, magic users raise an orb and hurl it, healers light those they mend; hits knock targets back and flash them, the fallen fall, the stunned have stars, and the camera drifts and leans in on each turn. The tiles keep the numbers, and update as each turn's hits land. A skill gets a callout banner over the stage. | "a 3d representation of the battle going on in between the current tiles... something final fantasy 13, where the characters move around the environment and actually cast their spells" |
| P14 | The timeline belongs to the core: each turn has a start, a moment its hits land and an end, by how it plays (charge, shoot, spell, support, dazed). The page runs one clock at the chosen speed and fires the tile updates, floating numbers and sounds as the clock passes those moments. Skip jumps the clock to the end. Without WebGL 2 the same clock plays the fight on the tiles alone. | keeps the stage and the tiles in step |
| P15 | Sound effects synthesized in the browser from recipes in `data/sounds.json` (Raiders' `sound.js`; white noise from the core's generator): a swing, a shot, a cast, a burst, hits that sound different with and against the edge, crits, heals, haste, slow, stun, a knockout, a callout chime, victory, defeat, level-up and button clicks. A mute button on the menu and the battle screen, remembered by the browser. No audio file ships. | "sound effects" |
| P16 | The tiles sit beside the stage, never under it: the middle tile's sideways stagger and the acting tile's sideways slide are gone (the acting tile grows slightly instead), and the tile columns stack above the stage. The browser check measures that no tile overlaps the stage. | "the lhs rhs tiles... slightly getting cut off by the fighting visualization window" |
| P17 | Nobody stands still, as in Final Fantasy XIII: before every turn the core (`stage.rs`, `plan_spots`) gives each unit a spot, and between turns every unit not being hit jogs there, up to 3.2 m, to line up on the foe its next turn hits, at its kind's range (melee about 3.8 m, magic 6 m, ranged 7 m) and an angle of its own, keeping to a loose lane and 1.7 m from anyone else. Ranged and magic units strafe while they shoot; melee units run in from wherever they are. A unit being hit holds its ground for that turn, so a blow finds it. The camera pulls back far enough to fit the floor on a narrow stage. Where units stand is drawing only: the fight's rules do not use it. | "the players in the viusalization to actually move around and try and get good angles on each other", "look at final fantasy 13" |
| P18 | Two movement skills, as new effects in the fight's rules. **Dodge**: a hit, then the caster steps aside, and the next blow aimed at it misses (no damage, no stun or slow). **Flank**: the caster runs round to the target's side and strikes, and the hit counts as with the edge whatever the kinds. Nine units get one in place of a skill that repeated another unit's (Rook, Vela, Smog Sage and Tagger dodge; Pip, Exo Ed, Static, Jetpack June and Biscuit flank), so every kind has both. On the stage a Dodge rolls clear after its blow and its caster has wisps until the dodge is spent; a missed blow meets a sidestep and a "Dodged!"; a Flank runs an arc to the target's side. Online protocol 3, because the rules changed. | "add new moves they can cast that allow them to move around the environment in order to dodge attacks or reposition for extra effectiveness" |
| P19 | The campaign screen shows the crew and a "Change crew and skills" button; Back on the crew screen returns to wherever it was opened from. | "the crew window should also be accessible from the campaign window" |
| P20 | Every standing unit on the stage wears its side's colour, as FF13 marks friend and foe: a blue (yours) or red (theirs) disc and ring at its feet and a spinning diamond over its head; the fallen lose them. The tiles take the same two colours (the YOU and THEM labels, a stripe down each tile's outer edge, the skill callout). The colours are in `stage.rs` (`TEAM`) and `style.css` (`--team-mine`, `--team-foe`), named together in both. | "its hard to tell which team each character is on as they are fighting as they move now" |
| P21 | A larger battle space. The floor grows from 12 by 9 m to 15 by 12 m (drawn 18 by 14), the starting places and lanes spread to match, units keep 2.3 m apart (from 1.7) and the middle gap is 4.6 m. Melee lines up about 4.5 m from its foe, magic 7.7 m, ranged 8.8 m. The camera frames where units can stand rather than the whole floor, and leans less toward the acting side so nobody leaves the frame. On the page the stage is taller on a taller window (520 to 680 px) and the tile columns a little narrower (190 to 250 px), so the stage itself is wider. Drawing only: no rule changed, and the online protocol stays 3. | "make the battle space a little larger, its now a bit crowded in there" |
